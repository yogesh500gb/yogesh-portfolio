SOCIAL MEDIA STRATEGY

A social media strategy built around real customer questions.

Stop filling the calendar for the sake of posting. Give every piece of content a job.

Stop filling the calendar for the sake of posting. Give every piece of content a job.

Start with the audience.

Who is deciding? What are they worried about? What would help them trust you? Collect actual customer questions from sales calls, comments, reviews and support conversations.

Choose three to five content pillars.

A local service business might use helpful answers, behind-the-scenes work, customer proof and clear offers. A restaurant might use dishes, experience, location and occasions. Keep the categories easy to maintain.

Write a useful opening.

Open with the reason the post matters. A concrete problem, question or detail beats a generic “we are excited to announce” line. Keep the rest of the message short.

Give every post a reasonable next step.

A post can teach, build trust or invite an enquiry. Not every post needs to sell. But a useful business profile should always make the service and contact route easy to find.

Measure the right signal.

Reach, saves, watch time, profile visits, enquiries and sales do different jobs. Choose a small set of measures and look for patterns, not a single viral post.

Improve the routine.

Use a content calendar and a simple creative review. Do more of what serves the audience. Retire formats that add effort but little value.

Three useful checks

  • Choose real customer questions as content topics.
  • Use one clear idea per post.
  • Review content as a system, not a popularity contest.

What to do next

Choose the highest-impact issue you can verify. Write down the current state, make one clear improvement and review what changed. A useful audit should lead to action, not a longer to-do list nobody owns.

COMMON QUESTIONS

A few quick answers.

How often should a small business post?

As often as it can produce useful content consistently. Quality and relevance matter more than arbitrary daily targets.

Do I need paid ads with social management?

Not always. Organic content and paid media can support each other, but they have different roles.
