ANALYTICS · PRACTICAL GUIDE

Conversion tracking explained without the complicated words.

Know which actions help you make better decisions.

Know which actions help you make better decisions. Good marketing is rarely about one magic setting. It is about clear thinking, useful evidence and the next practical move.

Decide what a conversion means.

For a local service, a completed enquiry may matter. For a restaurant, a reservation may matter. For an online shop, a purchase matters. A click on every button should not have the same weight.

Make events easy to check.

List the important customer actions. Confirm what happens in the browser. Check that a tracked event fires only when the intended action happens. If a form merely opens WhatsApp, it should not be counted as a successfully delivered message.

Understand different platforms.

Google Ads, Meta Ads and GA4 can report different numbers. Lookback windows, attribution models, browser limits and event definitions all play a role. A mismatch is not always a broken tag.

Use reports as a conversation.

Your weekly report should explain what changed, what might have caused it and what needs checking. A chart without a decision is decoration.

Protect customer privacy.

Use necessary consent and collect only what is needed. Avoid putting private customer information into analytics event URLs.

What to do next

  • Write down every conversion event and its purpose.
  • Test tracking on desktop and mobile.
  • Separate reported conversions from confirmed sales.

Keep the goal in view.

Ask what you want your customer to understand or do. Choose the simplest useful measurement. Then improve one step. A small, clear change you can learn from is worth more than a complicated plan nobody follows.
